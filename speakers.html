    Topic & Abstract
    Title
    Pareto Optimization for Subset Selection: Theories and Practical Algorithms
    Abstract

    Subset selection aims to select a subset from a total set of items for optimizing some given objective function while satisfying some constraints. It is NP-hard, and has applications in diverse areas, including machine learning, data mining, natural language processing, computer vision, information retrieval, etc. This tutorial will introduce a promising framework, Pareto optimization, for the subset selection problem. The main idea of Pareto optimization is to transform the subset selection problem into a bi-objective optimization problem, then employ a multi-objective evolutionary algorithm to solve it, and finally return the best feasible solution w.r.t. the original subset selection problem from the generated non-dominated solution set. We will show that it achieves the best-so-far theoretical and practical performances in several applications of subset selection. We will also introduce advanced variants of Pareto optimization for large-scale, noisy and dynamic subset selection. Finally, we will briefly review the recent progress on this topic.

    Topic & Abstract
    Title
    SpikeCV: High-Speed Spike Vision for Visual Reconstruction and Perception
    Abstract

    Spike cameras, inspired by the biological retina's integrate-and-fire mechanism, provide a novel approach to continuous visual sensing by capturing visual information as asynchronous binary spike streams at ultra-high temporal resolution. Unlike conventional frame-based cameras, spike cameras offer unique advantages in capturing high-speed motion and dynamic scenes, opening new opportunities for visual reconstruction and perception.


    This tutorial will introduce the fundamental principles, recent advances, and emerging applications of spike-based vision, with a particular focus on SpikeCV, an open-source platform for spike camera data processing and visual computing. We will first provide an overview of spike camera imaging mechanisms, spike data representations, and the SpikeCV framework, including its datasets, algorithms, and research ecosystem. We will then explore representative approaches to high-quality image and video reconstruction from spike streams, covering key challenges in recovering visual details, handling complex motion, and improving reconstruction quality. Furthermore, we will discuss how the rich temporal information captured by spike cameras can support high-speed visual perception and scene understanding. Finally, we will highlight current challenges and promising research directions toward efficient and continuous visual intelligence.


    This tutorial aims to provide researchers and students with a comprehensive introduction to spike-based vision, bridging neuromorphic sensing, computational imaging, and intelligent visual perception.

    Topic & Abstract
    Title
    Evolutionary Transfer Optimization: Theory, Methods, and Applications
    Abstract

    Evolutionary transfer optimization mines and reuses experiential knowledge from related optimization tasks, thereby breaking the "experience silo" dilemma caused by the "solving-from-scratch" paradigm of conventional evolutionary algorithms and significantly improving both the solution efficiency and solution quality of new tasks. However, real-world engineering optimization tasks, as exemplified by oil and gas field development, are generally characterized by strong nonlinearity, high uncertainty, and pronounced heterogeneity: reservoir numerical simulations are computationally expensive, geological parameters are subject to considerable uncertainty, and optimization problems across different blocks and development stages differ markedly in the dimensionality of decision variables and in their objective spaces. These characteristics give rise to prominent challenges, including difficulty in knowledge extraction, low transfer accuracy, and poor adaptability. This tutorial systematically reviews research progress in evolutionary transfer optimization from three perspectives: theory, methods, and applications. At the theoretical level, it introduces an analogical-reasoning-driven framework for transfer optimization and elucidates the underlying mechanisms of task similarity measurement and knowledge transferability. At the methodological level, it focuses on a series of methods including multi-source knowledge extraction, adaptive transfer, and cross-task mapping learning, addressing the core questions of "what to transfer, when to transfer, and how to transfer" between tasks. At the application level, it draws on typical oil and gas development scenarios such as well placement and injection–production parameter optimization to demonstrate the practical effectiveness of transfer optimization in reducing the number of numerical simulation calls and accelerating development-plan decision-making. The tutorial concludes with an outlook on the integration of evolutionary transfer optimization with deep learning and large models, with the aim of shifting the decision-making paradigm for complex problems from traditional trial-and-error toward intelligent experience inheritance.
