Prof. Yew-Soon Ong
Distinction IEEE Fellow, Fellow of the Singapore National Academy of Engineering
Affiliation Nanyang Technological University, Singapore
Title
Evolutionary Multitasking-Past Advances and Future Opportunities
Abstract

Inspired by the human ability to reuse knowledge across related tasks, evolutionary multitasking (EMT) has emerged as a paradigm for solving multiple optimization problems through shared search and knowledge transfer. Over the past decade, EMT has evolved from multifactorial evolutionary algorithms and implicit genetic transfer toward increasingly challenging settings, including expensive optimization, knowledge transfer across Pareto solutions, and continuously parameterized, potentially infinite task spaces. Today, rapid advances in AI are opening a new chapter for EMT. As AI moves toward broader generalization, optimization must increasingly discover diverse, novel, and complementary solutions rather than a single optimum. This talk will trace the evolution of EMT and explore emerging opportunities in AI for scientific discovery, physics-informed learning, target-guided search, and novelty and quality-diversity search, highlighting its potential role in enabling more general and adaptive AI systems.

Dr. Bernhard Sendhoff
Distinction IEEE Fellow
Affiliation Honda Research Institute Europe GmbH (Offenbach), Germany
Title
AI in Engineering Design: From Tools to Teams
Abstract

For many years AI particularly, computational intelligence, has been applied to the task of engineering design. I will start my talk by recalling some intelligent tools for modelling, optimization and data analysis that are integrated into the engineering design process. After stating remaining challenges for this "classical" approach, I will focus on using generative AI and large language models as new tools in the engineering domain. Text prompts for shape generation and concept identification are two directions that I will discuss.


In the second part of my presentation, I will introduce cooperative intelligence as a variant of AI that focuses on the interaction between humans and machine intelligence. The science of cooperation is embedded in psychology, social and behavioral science and not least in evolution. It must be augmented by human- AI interaction. We will present first research results on measuring successful cooperation beyond performance.


Engineering design for complex systems is organized in teams. Therefore, cooperative intelligence must not be restricted to dyadic interactions but extended to groups and their complex social dynamics.


Finally, hybrid teams composed of humans and AI agents will not only be human teams augmented by AI but will be new organizational units where agentic AI will form own cooperative sub- units that can explore design spaces semi- autonomously and proactively while concurrently participating in team discussions and conclusions. Engineering innovation will be more disruptive, and faster with the human perspective on integration and interoperability.

Biography

He is Honorary Professor at the Technical University of Darmstadt, Germany. Bernhard Sendhoff is a Fellow of the IEEE, a Senior Member of the ACM, and a Member of the SAE. He has authored or co-authored more than 200 peer reviewed journal and conference papers and over 40 patents and has a Google Scholar h-index of 54.

Prof. Gary G. Yen
Distinction IEEE Fellow, IET Fellow, IAPR Fellow
Affiliation Sichuan University, China
Title
Generative Model-based Large-scale Dynamic Multiobjective Optimization
Abstract

Large-scale, dynamic multi-objective optimization problems (LSDMOPs) extend traditional DMOPs into high-dimensional decision spaces, reflecting the growing complexity of real-world dynamic systems under expected or unanticipated environmental changes. A typical example is the economic control of gas transportation in large-scale pipeline network, which involve hundreds or even thousands of decision variables, representing pipeline node pressures, compressor power settings, and valve operations. The system is simultaneously affected by various time-varying factors, such as consumer demand, market fluctuation, environmental temperature and humidity. In such contexts, algorithms are required to quickly find optimal solutions after each environment change, maintaining supply-demand balance while minimizing energy consumption. In practice, there are many more such examples, including dynamic resource allocation of 5G networks in dense urban environments, UAV swarm dispatch in a disaster relief scenario, and large-scale dynamic vehicle routing planning, just to name a few.


However, the effectiveness of existing dynamic multi-objective evolutionary algorithms is severely limited for LSDMOPs, due to inadequate training data, predictions in unknown environments, and large-scale dynamic search spaces. To address these challenges, we propose a diffusion learning-based evolutionary framework, inspired by the intrinsic analogy between iterative evolution of optimization search and stepwise denoising in diffusion learning. Specifically, a new training paradigm is designed to learn the changing patterns of optimal regions in dynamic fitness landscapes. It achieves this by using populations’ evolutionary trajectories from initial solutions towards Pareto-optimal solutions across historical environments as rich supervised training data. In addition, we introduce a trajectory alignment loss which encourages the stepwise denoising process to conform to the true population evolutionary behaviors in terms of spatial exploration, convergence trends, and boundary adaptation. The trained model can gradually control denoising direction and intensity using predefined conditions, allowing it to generate optimization paths from random noise toward Pareto-optimal solutions for a new environment.


Along with an adversarial autoencoder-based large-scale dynamic multi-objective evolutionary framework and a large language model-based large-scale dynamic multi-objective evolutionary framework, we will assess how deep generative modeling techniques and large-scale multi-objective evolutionary algorithms can be seamlessly integrated to solve large-scale DMOPs effectively and efficiently. Experimental results on a typical dynamic multi-objective test suite with problem settings from 10 to 1,000 dimensions demonstrate that the optimization performance of the proposed framework outperforms existing state-of-the-art designs. Especially in large-scale scenarios, the proposed framework is considered superior in terms of solution quality and computational efficiency.

Prof. Jose A. Lozano
Distinction IEEE Fellow
Affiliation University of the Basque Country (UPV/EHU) & Basque Center for Applied Mathematics (BCAM), Spain
Title
The Essence of Combinatorial Optimization Problems: From Fourier Structure to Rankings and Algorithms
Abstract

What is the essential structure of a combinatorial optimization problem? Objective functions provide a natural description, but may hide common structures between apparently different problems and the features that determine algorithmic behaviour. In this talk, I will present Fourier analysis as a common language for understanding combinatorial optimization problems. I will discuss Fourier representations of classical permutation-based problems such as TSP, LOP, and QAP, showing how they reveal intrinsic dimensionality, equivalence between instances, and relationships between problem classes. I will then move from objective functions to the rankings they induce, which are the information used by many optimization algorithms. Recent results extending Chow’s theorem to rankings provide a spectral characterization of this information, including for functions defined over permutations. Finally, I will discuss how these structural insights can influence algorithmic behaviour, using the Fourier decomposition of the Linear Ordering Problem as an example.

Prof. Xiangjian He
Affiliation The University of Nottingham-Ningbo, China
Title
Towards Generalizable, Lightweight, and Trustworthy Medical Artificial Intelligence
Abstract

Medical imaging is the cornerstone of disease screening, diagnosis, and prognostic assessment, and artificial intelligence, represented by vision foundation models, is profoundly reshaping clinical workflows. However, translating medical AI into real-world clinical practice still faces three major challenges. First, high-quality annotated data are scarce, and the imaging process itself can impose an additional radiation burden. Second, large models incur high computational costs and generalize poorly across modalities and clinical centers. Third, the trustworthiness and interpretability of diagnostic results remain to be improved. This talk is organized around three stages: data, analysis, and diagnosis. At the data level, we build the first public ultrasound segmentation dataset for cesarean scar defects, and propose a CT-free PET attenuation and scatter correction method based on few-shot cross-domain adaptation, which adapts to new clinical settings with only a handful of samples. At the analysis level, we combine parameter-efficient fine-tuning, knowledge distillation, frequency-guided multi-task routing, and frozen foundation model adaptation to achieve lightweight and generalizable medical image analysis. At the diagnosis level, we leverage multi-agent collaborative verification and hyperbolic multimodal learning to improve the accuracy and trustworthiness of pathological diagnosis and survival prediction.

Biography

Xiangjian He, an Australian citizen with a PhD in Computer Science, is Chair Professor of Computer Science and Technology, Discipline Leader, and Director of the Computer Vision and Intelligent Perception Laboratory at the University of Nottingham Ningbo China. He received his PhD in Computer Science from the University of Technology Sydney, Australia, in 1999. He has been selected for a national high-level talent program and as a Zhejiang Provincial Key Talent, and has been named in Stanford University's list of the World's Top 2% Scientists for consecutive years.


Professor He has long worked on computer vision, machine learning, medical image analysis, and intelligent perception. He has published more than 500 papers in leading international journals such as IEEE TPAMI and ACM Computing Surveys and at major conferences including CVPR, ICCV, and AAAI, with over 16,000 citations on Google Scholar. Before returning to China, he led or participated in 36 research and industrialization projects with total funding equivalent to more than RMB 20 million, including over RMB 10 million from industry collaborations.


Since joining the University of Nottingham Ningbo China full-time in 2022, Professor He has led the development of Computer Science and Technology as a Ningbo Excellent Distinctive Discipline (Category A), established the Computer Vision and Intelligent Perception (CVIP) research team, and advanced the building of an international research platform and talent development system. Focusing on healthcare, urban safety, and the digital economy, he has led research projects such as intelligent detection in breast cancer pathology images, conducted pathology image analysis and computer-aided diagnosis research with medical institutions, and worked with local companies on the development and commercialization of technologies including intelligent video processing and intelligent inspection of rail transit bridges. In recent years, the team has also published important findings in Nature Portfolio journals such as npj Digital Medicine and Scientific Data.


In 2026, a student team led by Professor He won first place worldwide in the Surgical Action Triplet Recognition challenge at the 29th International Conference on Medical Image Computing and Computer Assisted Intervention (MICCAI 2026), the first time a Chinese university has won this challenge. The result demonstrates the team's research strength in medical artificial intelligence and surgical video understanding.


In addition, Professor He has organized international conferences in Ningbo, including VSIP 2024 and CVIDL 2025, which attracted nearly 500 attendances from experts and scholars in China and abroad. He has supervised 37 PhD graduates and 4 postdoctoral researchers, and continues to promote international academic collaboration, high-level talent development, and the industrial application of artificial intelligence technologies.
